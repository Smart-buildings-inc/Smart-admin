## 1. Positioning discipline

### Category

**ATLAS OS is a resilience infrastructure operating layer for buildings and portfolios.**

Do not lead with:
- futuristic habitat
- self-sufficient building dream
- 3D twin as the headline
- AI novelty

Lead with:
- lower commissioning risk
- faster operational visibility
- better portfolio control
- auditable resilience and ESG performance
- measurable operating economics

### One-line positioning

**ATLAS OS turns a building from a handover event into a live, measurable operating asset on day one.**

### Proof points already visible in the product

- live digital twin for whole-building visibility
- per-floor telemetry and incident triage
- resident broadcast with operational audit trail
- fleet rollup across multiple assets
- auditable water, energy, air, and resilience signals
- local-first operating model with seed-data fallback
- clear control boundary: AI advises; deterministic controls and humans hold life safety

## 2. Ideal buyers

### Portfolio operator version

### City and housing authority version

We help public-sector housing teams move resilience from a policy claim into a live operating system with measurable water, energy, air, and resident communication performance.

## 5. Discovery questions

Use these to qualify budget, urgency, and contract shape.

1. Where do building performance and operations currently become visible for you: during design, at handover, or only after problems appear?
2. How many assets or projects would need to be visible in one operating layer for this to matter?
3. Which of these hurts most today: commissioning friction, incident response, utility performance, ESG proof, or portfolio visibility?
4. Who owns the budget when building performance slips: development, operations, asset management, or sustainability?
5. Is there a flagship project, pilot property, or public funding program that makes this urgent in the next 6 to 12 months?
6. If we could prove this on one development or a small portfolio slice, what would expansion look like?

## 6. Objection handling

### "We already have a BMS."

That is normal. ATLAS is not trying to replace deterministic controls. It sits above them as the operating and measurement layer leadership and operators actually use.

### "This sounds early."

The point is not to buy a moonshot. The point is to buy earlier operating visibility, better commissioning control, and auditable resilience performance on a defined scope.

### "We are not building self-sufficient towers."

That is fine. You do not need the full ATLAS habitat thesis to benefit from the operating layer. The commercial wedge is visibility, response, and measurable performance across real assets.

### "We do not have budget for another software tool."

Then the entry point is not "another tool." It is a development-risk or resilience workstream with software embedded into a funded program.

### "Send something."

Good. Send the [Enterprise Offer Sheet](./ENTERPRISE_OFFER_SHEET.md), then follow within 24 hours with a tailored note that maps ATLAS to one real asset, portfolio, or public program they already own.

## 7. Meeting objective

The first meeting is not a product demo.

The first meeting should end with one of these:
- scoped paid discovery
- pilot-building workshop
- portfolio assessment
- public-sector resilience concept note

If the meeting ends with "interesting technology," the positioning failed.

## 9. Today's call list

Prioritize people with budget, an active project, and an operational problem.

1. developers with a flagship project inside 18 months of handover

## 10. Message discipline checklist

Before every call, confirm:

- the buyer has a budget owner, not just an innovation title
- the first sentence names the economic problem
- the demo, if shown, supports an operating case
- the ask is for a working session tied to a real asset or program
- the follow-up maps to a contract shape, not a vague future collaboration
