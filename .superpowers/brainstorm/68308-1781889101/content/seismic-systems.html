Choose the ATLAS seismic-resilience system

All options target post-disaster operation. The difference is how the structure limits damage and protects critical systems.

A · Conventional

Ductile concrete core

Strong, code-familiar, and economical. The building survives through ductility, but greater drift can damage façades, partitions, equipment, and utility connections.

B · Isolation

Base-isolated habitat

Excellent acceleration reduction for sensitive equipment. It needs a seismic moat, flexible service connections, wind checks, specialist procurement, and site-specific displacement design.

DAMPERS
C · Recommended

Hybrid resilient frame

Ductile reinforced-concrete core plus perimeter moment/braced frames and replaceable energy-dissipation devices. Lower drift and repair time without making base isolation mandatory on every site.

Why C fits ATLAS

  • Adapts to Toronto, Montréal, and Québec City hazard profiles.
  • Protects façades, stairs, elevators, MEP risers, water, power, and clinic equipment.
  • Allows replaceable dampers or buckling-restrained braces after a severe event.
  • Keeps base isolation available as a site-specific upgrade.

Engineering conditions

  • Geotechnical report and site class are mandatory.
  • Performance-based nonlinear analysis is expected.
  • Final member sizes and devices require licensed engineers.
  • Non-structural restraint is part of the seismic system, not an add-on.
